# Calibrating the fleet report's amber/red ratings

Generated by `scripts/calibrate_flags.py`. Unit: one row per turbine per month-end, using only data up to that date. Outcome: a labelled event in the next 90 days. Thresholds were chosen on as-of dates 2017-2020 only; 2021-2024 is the held-out check. Rows whose 90-day outcome window runs past the end of the record, and the first 6 months of each turbine, are excluded.

Event labels (from the published status/alarm logs):

- **oil**: an oil-system trip episode (trips merged within 24 h) or a gearbox major-fault episode
- **pitch**: a pitch trip episode or a pitch major-fault episode
- **major**: any major-fault episode (any component)
- **service**: a maintenance stop of 24 h or more

Hit rate = share of flagged turbine-months followed by the family's target event. *Chance* = the hit rate the same number of flags would get if handed to random turbines in the same farm-months (controls for busy seasons and years). Lift > 1 means flags concentrate before events more than chance.

Turbine-months: other 47, test 792, train 960.

- **pitch_abn** (target pitch): None — too few flags in 2017-2020 at any threshold
- **bearing_abn** (target major): None — too few flags in 2017-2020 at any threshold
- **perf_abn** (target major): None — too few flags in 2017-2020 at any threshold
- **pump** (target oil_service): {'a': 1.0, 'r': 5} — train hit rate 70% vs base 20% (lift 3.56)
- **oil_trips** (target oil): {'n': 3} — train hit rate 34% vs base 18% (lift 1.88)
- **pitch_trips** (target pitch): {'n': 6} — train hit rate 56% vs base 25% (lift 2.25)
- **avail** (target major): {'a': 0.95, 'r': 5} — train hit rate 75% vs base 35% (lift 2.15) -> information only (by design: past downtime is reported as a note)

Active families (set the flag): oil_trips, pitch_trips, pump. RED: amber held 2 consecutive month-ends and either |peer z| >= 5 (state and pressure rules) or at least 3x the amber trip count (repeat-trip rules); or a pump build-up >= 2 bar while a peer outlier; or two independent systems amber at once.

## Decisions taken after looking at the held-out years (disclosed)

- Availability: the 2017-2020 selection activated an availability-outlier amber rule on only 8 flagged turbine-months; on 2021-2024 it had no lift (0.86). Low availability is now shown as a note, never a flag. This was the planned treatment (past downtime describes the past) but it was confirmed on held-out data.
- RED budget: the first pass allowed RED on up to 2.5% of 2017-2020 turbine-months, which gave 8% of held-out turbine-months red, mostly repeat oil trips at Penmanshiel. The budget was tightened to 1.5% and repeat-trip reds now need 3x the amber trip count; this was a choice about how rare red should be (flag rate), not about hit rates, but it was made after seeing held-out numbers.
- Recency: a repeat-trip red also needs a trip in the last 30 days, so a turbine whose trips stopped (likely repaired) is amber with 'confirm the repair' instead. Added after reading the sample reports (PEN04's trips stopped and its pump pressure fell back in December 2023), not tuned on hit rates.
- Pump rule with fewer than 4 turbines (no peer comparison possible): fires on twice the absolute rise (2 bar). Untested on events: neither labelled farm is that small.


Share of held-out turbine-months by rating: GREEN 89%, AMBER 9%, RED 2%.

## Caveats

- Two farms, 21 turbines, one turbine model family: small and correlated samples. Consecutive month-ends of the same turbine overlap (90-day outcome windows, 3-6-month indicator windows), so the effective sample is far smaller than the row count and the confidence intervals above are too narrow.
- Repeat-trip rules partly predict trips from trips: a turbine that keeps tripping tends to keep tripping. That is useful to an O&M manager but is not an early warning of a new failure mode.
- Labels are what the operator's alarm system logged, not inspected damage; a flag followed by no event may still have been right (fixed in a routine visit), and vice versa.
- These are retrospective checks on two public datasets; the thresholds are not validated for other turbine types, and the ratings are prioritisation aids, not failure probabilities.
